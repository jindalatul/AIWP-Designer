description: Decide what the page is before deciding how it looks.

Before any markup, write down (briefly, as structured values, not an essay):

- `page_goal` — what the business wants this page to achieve
- `audience` — who arrives, and what they already believe
- `primary_conversion` — the one action that matters
- `visual_direction` — three to six words naming the intended feel
- `story` — the ordered beats the visitor moves through

Then choose sections that serve those beats. Let the story decide the sections; never pick sections first and fit content into them.

A page that argues something needs a different shape from a page that lists something. Some real shapes:

- a claim, the proof for it, the objection, the answer, the ask
- a problem told in one long editorial block, then a single decisive CTA
- a comparison table flanked by two short narratives
- a numbered process with one wide image per step
- a data story: three numbers, then what they mean
- a single long-form explanation with a sticky summary rail

Aim for four to eight sections. More than eight usually means two pages.

Give each section a job in one sentence. If two sections have the same job, cut one.
